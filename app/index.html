      How BiteCast works

      Every waterway gets a score from 0 to 100: four factors multiplied together, so the weakest one caps the score. No trained model; every number can be checked against its source.

      Mosquito growth
      development
      Warmth banked by larvae in the water, day by day, measured in the laboratory for each species. When enough has built up, adults emerge. Development slows again in extreme heat, and adults then live about a week.
      Still water
      stagnation
      Days since rain last flushed the larvae out. Small channels flush at 10 mm in a day, ponds and basins only at 25 mm.
      Water type
      habitat
      How likely the water is to stand still. Wastewater and stormwater basins, ponds, ditches and drains score high; flowing streams and rivers low. Fountains, swimming pools, tanks and culverted reaches are left out.
      People nearby
      exposure
      Homes, parks, playgrounds and schools within 300 m. Children's places count double.

      Where winters are cold, the season ends once days drop below 12 hours and the weekly mean below 15 °C: new females then overwinter instead of biting (Field et al. 2022).

      The season chart

      The bars along the bottom of the map are the year, one bar per week. Each bar is how bad the worst spots in the place are that week, in the same colours as the map. The striped bars are the forecast, the outlined ones are weeks not known yet, and the blue marks underneath are rain: heavy rain flushes larvae out of ditches and drains. Drag across the chart, or tap a week, to see the map on that day.

      Which mosquito

      Each place gets the species that actually live there, chosen from real GBIF records within 250 km and from whether the local winter lets each one survive. Every species runs on its own laboratory-measured curves for growth, larval survival and lifespan.

      Culex pipiens
      northern house mosquito
      Europe's West Nile vector, breeding in drains, ditches and ponds; bites at dusk and night. Loetti et al. 2011.
      Culex quinquefasciatus
      southern house mosquito
      The house mosquito of warm countries: the same kind of water, no winter dormancy. Shocket et al. 2020.
      Aedes aegypti
      dengue mosquito
      Breeds in containers such as buckets, drums, tanks and tyres, and bites in the daytime. Cannot survive a winter colder than about 10 °C. Mordecai et al. 2017.
      Aedes albopictus
      Asian tiger mosquito
      A container breeder that has spread across southern Europe; bites in the daytime. Mordecai et al. 2017.

      Water comes from two sources. OpenStreetMap knows each ditch, drain and pond by name and type; satellites (JRC Global Surface Water, 1984–2021) add the ponds and seasonal pools nobody has mapped, and say how many months a year each holds water. People nearby are counted from Meta's satellite-based population map where it covers the place, so a town with few homes on the map still counts its people. A species is modelled where it has been recorded within 250 km, or where the climate suits it and the area has too few mosquito records to rule it out.

      No map shows buckets or tyres, so the container breeders' risk is drawn on the mapped homes, schools, parks and playgrounds: rain fills the containers, evaporation empties them, and some water is always stored.

      Any place on earth

      Search any town, use your location, or zoom in on any town: BiteCast fetches that area's water, weather and mosquito records the first time and keeps them, so the next visit is instant. The model is checked most thoroughly on five river basins studied by the OneAquaHealth project, the places in the charts above.

      Standards

      Each forecast can be exported as a FHIR R4 Observation whose subject is a Location, the water feature itself. There is no LOINC code for mosquito emergence risk, so the code is local and clearly namespaced. Standard vector-surveillance codes are a gap.

      Your reports and your privacy

      A report stores the water feature, the date, your yes or no, and a random ID your browser keeps so a second tap replaces the first. No name, no account, no location. Reports are compared with what the model predicted, and that comparison is shown on the map: it is how the model gets checked.

      Limits

      Air temperature stands in for water temperature. Flushing is all-or-nothing. The rates come from laboratory populations. Containers are not mapped, so container breeders are drawn on neighbourhoods, not on the buckets themselves.
